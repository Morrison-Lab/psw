This chapter lists the book’s main rules as a checklist. Use the checklist when you revise a draft: read the draft once for each group of rules, and fix each passage that breaks a rule. Each rule is stated in a few words here. Follow its link for the reasons and the exceptions.

## 1 Terms and notation

- [Define every technical term](../chapters/defining-terms.llms.md#guidelines-for-defining-terms) explicitly, next to its first use, and follow the definition with examples and counterexamples.
- [Give each mathematical statement an example](../chapters/defining-terms.llms.md#mathematical-statements-require-examples) that applies it.
- [Prefer interval notation to a double inequality](../chapters/notation.llms.md#avoiding-double-inequalities): \\x \in (a, b)\\ rather than \\a \< x \< b\\.
- [Do one operation per step](../chapters/notation.llms.md#one-operation-per-step) in a derivation, and justify each step.
- [Give applying a definition its own step](../chapters/notation.llms.md#applying-a-definition-is-a-step); never skip it.
- [Show why you need a result before you derive it](../chapters/notation.llms.md#show-why-you-need-a-result-before-you-derive-it), and [color each result where it is used](../chapters/notation.llms.md#color-where-a-result-is-used).
- [Put one equals sign on each line](../chapters/notation.llms.md#one-equals-sign-per-line): split a chain such as \\a = b = c\\ into the rows of an aligned display.
- [Distinguish a function from its value](../chapters/notation.llms.md#functions-and-their-values).
- [Use the notation that matches a quantity’s name](../chapters/notation.llms.md#matching-names-and-notation), and keep the pairing the same everywhere.
- [Write each symbol through a shared macro](../chapters/notation.llms.md#using-shared-macros).
- [Write out notational shorthands](../chapters/notation.llms.md#writing-out-notational-shorthands), such as the limits of a sum.

## 2 Claims and evidence

- [Support every claim](../chapters/citations-evidence.llms.md#why-claims-need-support) with a citation or with data.
- [Do not substitute flippancy for support](../chapters/citations-evidence.llms.md#do-not-substitute-flippancy-for-support), such as calling a step “trivial”.
- [Cite a relevant source](../chapters/citations-evidence.llms.md#what-makes-a-citation-relevant) that is [trustworthy](../chapters/citations-evidence.llms.md#what-makes-a-source-trustworthy).
- [Copy exact wording with caution](../chapters/citations-evidence.llms.md#copy-paste-with-caution), even from your own earlier work.
- [Link liberally](../chapters/citations-evidence.llms.md#link-liberally) to the sources and tools that you mention.
- [Quote the original](../chapters/citations-evidence.llms.md#quote-the-original) rather than a source that quotes it.
- [Put the content first](../chapters/citations-evidence.llms.md#put-the-content-first): name the topic, not its author, in headings and sentences.

## 3 Papers

- [Follow the standard structure](../chapters/paper-organization.llms.md#standard-paper-structure) of a research paper.
- [Organize the Results section](../chapters/paper-organization.llms.md#organizing-the-results-section) to fit the design of the study.
- [Label variables in plain words](../chapters/paper-organization.llms.md#label-variables-in-plain-words) in figures and tables.
- [Use descriptive subsection headings](../chapters/paper-organization.llms.md#subsection-headings).
- [Put each link to a video next to the text that it supports](../chapters/paper-organization.llms.md#links-to-videos-and-other-media).
- Follow the [journal’s conventions for a manuscript](../chapters/manuscript-submission.llms.md), and check the [submission checklist](../chapters/manuscript-submission.llms.md#submission-checklist) before you submit.

## 4 Words

- [Prefer plain words to Latin-derived ones](../chapters/word-choice.llms.md) where a plain word says the same thing.
- [Avoid vague and metaphorical language](../chapters/word-choice.llms.md#avoid-vague-and-metaphorical-language).
- [Follow an abstract statement with an example](../chapters/word-choice.llms.md#follow-an-abstract-statement-with-an-example).
- [Replace the word “thing”](../chapters/word-choice.llms.md#say-which-thing) with the object, quantity, step, or idea that it stands for.
- [Minimize unnecessary jargon](../chapters/word-choice.llms.md#minimize-unnecessary-jargon).
- [Edit out clusters of the words and sentence shapes](../chapters/avoid-ai-tells.llms.md) that mark machine-drafted prose.

## 5 Writing for every reader

- [Use literal language](../chapters/inclusive-writing.llms.md#use-literal-language).
- [Use common words, and define technical ones](../chapters/inclusive-writing.llms.md#use-common-words-and-define-technical-ones).
- [Say when a common word has a technical meaning](../chapters/inclusive-writing.llms.md#say-when-a-common-word-has-a-technical-meaning), such as “significant” or “bias”.
- [Spell out abbreviations](../chapters/inclusive-writing.llms.md#spell-out-abbreviations).
- [Write positive statements](../chapters/inclusive-writing.llms.md#write-positive-statements), and [avoid double negatives](../chapters/conciseness.llms.md#avoid-double-negatives).
- [Rewrite noun strings and hidden verbs](../chapters/inclusive-writing.llms.md#rewrite-noun-strings-and-hidden-verbs).
- [Put one idea in each sentence](../chapters/inclusive-writing.llms.md#put-one-idea-in-each-sentence).
- [State every step and every point](../chapters/inclusive-writing.llms.md#state-every-step-and-every-point).
- [Say what numbers and symbols mean](../chapters/inclusive-writing.llms.md#say-what-numbers-and-symbols-mean).
- [Use examples that every reader knows](../chapters/inclusive-writing.llms.md#use-examples-that-every-reader-knows).
- [Keep a predictable structure](../chapters/inclusive-writing.llms.md#keep-a-predictable-structure).
- [Present ideas in more than one form](../chapters/inclusive-writing.llms.md#present-ideas-in-more-than-one-form).
- [Test your writing with a reader](../chapters/inclusive-writing.llms.md#test-your-writing-with-a-reader).

## 6 Sentences

- [Keep relative pronouns](../chapters/grammar.llms.md#relative-pronouns) such as “that” and “which”.
- [Follow “this” or “that” with the noun that it refers to](../chapters/grammar.llms.md#demonstrative-pronouns).
- [Follow a quantifier such as “both” or “each” with its noun](../chapters/grammar.llms.md#quantifiers-used-as-pronouns).
- [Avoid garden-path sentences](../chapters/grammar.llms.md#avoid-garden-path-sentences).
- [Name the object of a relational noun](../chapters/grammar.llms.md#name-the-object-of-a-relational-noun).
- [Lead with the subject](../chapters/conciseness.llms.md#lead-with-the-subject).
- [Limit complex sentence structures](../chapters/conciseness.llms.md#limit-complex-sentence-structures).
- [Do not rule out readings that no reader would make](../chapters/conciseness.llms.md#do-not-rule-out-readings-no-reader-would-make).
- [Write about the subject, not about the text](../chapters/conciseness.llms.md#write-about-the-subject-not-about-the-text).
- [Put a list of three or more phrases in bullet points](../chapters/conciseness.llms.md#put-lists-in-bullet-points).
